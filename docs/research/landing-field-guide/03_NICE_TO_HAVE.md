# 03 — Nice-to-Have Components (Complements)

## 1. Comparison Section

### When it's worth it
- Crowded categories where visitors are actively comparing (CRMs, project tools, payment providers).
- When you're replacing a "do it manually" habit (vs. spreadsheets, vs. agencies, vs. hiring).
- Dedicated "[You] vs [Competitor]" pages capture high-intent search traffic.

### Job
Make the decision **easier** by framing the comparison on the dimensions where you win — honestly.

### Psychology
- **Framing:** whoever defines the comparison criteria shapes the conclusion.
- **Credibility through concession:** admitting where a competitor is better ("Choose X if you need Y") makes all your other claims more believable.

### Content rules
- Compare **3–8 decision-relevant criteria**, not 40 features.
- Option A: you vs. "the old way" (spreadsheets, agencies, doing nothing) — safest and often most persuasive.
- Option B: you vs. named competitors — powerful but must be accurate, dated ("Last updated: March 2026") and verifiable.
- Include at least one criterion where you **don't** win, or a "Who should choose [competitor]" line.

### Layout
- Table with your column highlighted (accent background or border), check/cross icons plus short text (icons alone are ambiguous and inaccessible).
- On mobile: a sticky first column, or switch to stacked "card per competitor" layout.

### 🔴 Critical errors
- False or outdated claims about competitors. In the EU, comparative advertising must be objective and verifiable; misleading comparisons create legal exposure.

### 🟠 Common errors
- Every row a green check for you and a red cross for them (reads as propaganda).
- Comparing on features nobody cares about.

### 💎 Insider notes
- "Migration" content beside the comparison ("Switch in an afternoon — we import your data for free") converts better than the comparison itself; it removes the switching cost, which is usually the real objection.

## 2. Live Chat / Chatbot

### When it's worth it
- B2B, high-ticket or complex offers where a pre-sales question can unblock a deal.
- When you can staff it (or the bot can hand off to a human fast).
- **Not worth it** on low-ticket B2C landing pages or pages meant to drive a single simple action — it's a distraction and a heavy script.

### Job
Answer the one question that stands between a qualified visitor and the CTA.

### Rules
- **Don't pop open automatically** in the first seconds. If you use proactive messages, trigger them on intent signals (time on pricing page > 30 s, second visit, scrolling back up to pricing).
- Show expected response time ("Typically replies in 5 min").
- AI chatbots must be clearly identified as AI and offer a route to a human.
- The chat bubble must not cover the mobile sticky CTA, the cookie banner buttons, or form submit buttons.

### 🔴 Critical errors
- A chat widget loading 500 KB+ of JavaScript on page load and destroying INP/LCP.
- A bot that loops without ever reaching a human.

### 💎 Insider notes
- Load the chat script **on interaction** (on click of a lightweight fake bubble, or after a delay/idle) instead of on page load. Same functionality, a fraction of the performance cost.
- Chat transcripts are a gold mine: export them monthly and turn repeated questions into FAQ items and hero microcopy.

## 3. Interactive Demo, Calculator or Quiz

### When it's worth it
- **Interactive product demo** (clickable tour): PLG SaaS where "seeing is believing" but signup has friction. Navattic reports cases of large free-trial lifts after adding interactive demo CTAs.
- **ROI / savings calculator:** when the value is financial and depends on the visitor's numbers.
- **Quiz / product finder:** when there are several products or plans and visitors don't know which suits them (beauty, insurance, courses, mattresses).

### Psychology
- **IKEA effect / self-persuasion:** results people calculate themselves are more convincing than claims you make.
- **Personalisation:** a personalised result ("You'd save €4,320/year") feels relevant in a way generic claims can't.
- **Micro-commitments:** each answer in a quiz is a small yes.

### Rules
- Calculator: 2–4 inputs max, sensible defaults pre-filled, the result updates live, and the result block ends with a CTA ("Start saving €4,320/year").
- Quiz: 3–7 questions, a progress bar, an honest result, and **don't gate the result** behind an email unless the value is clearly worth it (offer "email me my results" as optional).
- Interactive demo: guided hotspots, 5–10 steps, skippable, ending with a CTA.

### 🔴 Critical errors
- Calculators with inflated assumptions that produce unbelievable results (destroys credibility instantly).
- Quiz results that always recommend the most expensive option.

### 💎 Insider notes
- Show the formula/assumptions behind the calculator in a collapsible "How we calculate this". Transparency is what makes the number persuasive.
- Calculators and quizzes are excellent **lead qualification** tools: the inputs tell sales exactly who the lead is.

## 4. Announcement Bar

### When it's worth it
Launches, time-limited offers, important news (funding, major feature, event).

### Rules
- One line, ≤ 80 characters, with a single link ("New: AI reports are here →").
- 36–48 px tall; dismissible (×) with the dismissal remembered.
- Reserve its height in the layout from the start so it doesn't push content down when it loads (layout shift).

### 🟠 Common errors
- Permanent announcement bars that are never updated (banner blindness; signals neglect).
- Stacking an announcement bar + cookie banner + sticky header + chat bubble — the mobile viewport becomes 40% chrome.

### 💎 Insider notes
- An announcement bar about a **recent** event (a launch this month) is also a freshness signal: it tells visitors the company is active.

## 5. Founder / Team / Story Block

### When it's worth it
Small brands, services, consultancies, DTC products, mission-driven companies, B2B startups selling to early adopters.

### Psychology
- **Liking & similarity:** people buy from people. A real face and an honest story create connection that logos can't.
- **Accountability:** a named founder signals that someone is responsible.

### Rules
- Real photo (not a stock portrait), name, role, a 60–120 word story focused on **why** the product exists and what it means for the customer.
- Optional: signature, personal email, link to a LinkedIn profile.

### 🟠 Common errors
- Long company history nobody asked for.
- "Our mission is to empower…" corporate language that cancels out the human effect.

### 💎 Insider notes
- A short founder video (30–60 s, phone-quality is fine) often outperforms polished brand videos for early-stage products because it signals authenticity.

## 6. Integrations Section

### When it's worth it
SaaS and tools that connect to an ecosystem.

### Rules
- Grid of recognisable logos of the tools your audience already uses, a count ("200+ integrations"), and a link to the full directory.
- Put the **3–5 most important** integrations first and larger.

### 💎 Insider notes
- This section doubles as social proof ("this fits into my world") and as an objection killer ("Does it work with what I use?"). Consider moving it higher for technical audiences.

## 7. Exit-Intent & Scroll-Triggered Offers

### When it's worth it
E-commerce and lead-gen pages with significant traffic and a genuinely valuable secondary offer (discount, lead magnet, waitlist).

### Rules
- Trigger on **exit intent** (desktop) or **meaningful engagement** (e.g. 50–70% scroll, 30+ seconds), never on page load.
- One-time per session/visitor; easy to close (visible ×, Esc key, click outside).
- The offer must be **different** from the main CTA (a downgrade path: "Not ready? Get the free guide").

### 🔴 Critical errors
- Pop-ups on page load on mobile (intrusive interstitials harm user experience and are discouraged by Google for mobile search).
- "Confirmshaming" close links ("No thanks, I prefer losing money") — a recognised dark pattern; increasingly targeted by EU regulators under consumer and digital services rules.
- Modals without keyboard focus management (focus trapped incorrectly, or not trapped at all).

### 💎 Insider notes
- On mobile, exit intent doesn't exist (no cursor). "Back-button" or "scroll up quickly" heuristics are unreliable; a bottom slide-in after engagement is less intrusive than a full modal.

## 8. Gamified & Personalised Elements

### When it's worth it
Consumer brands, education, community products, and any product where engagement and return visits matter. Figma's 2026 trends describe gamification — points, levels, progress bars, challenges, micro-rewards — as a way to make complex flows feel rewarding, citing Duolingo and Sephora.

### Options
- Progress bars in multi-step signup ("You're 2 steps from your plan").
- Small rewards for completing an action (unlocking a discount after a quiz).
- Dynamic content by location, referral source, or industry (e.g. "Popular with teams in Madrid").

### Rules
- Personalisation must be **accurate** (wrong-city personalisation is worse than none) and **not creepy** (don't show what you know about the visitor beyond the obvious).
- Gamification must support the conversion goal, not compete with it.

### 💎 Insider notes
- The highest-ROI personalisation is usually the most boring: **matching the headline to the ad or keyword** that brought the visitor. Do that before investing in anything fancier.
