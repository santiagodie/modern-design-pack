# Page-type matrix

Reference for `modern-design`. A safety net, not a starting point: consult it after reasoning (`reasoning.md`) and writing the brief, to check whether you missed a typical doubt for this kind of page. When reasoning you can explain in one sentence contradicts the matrix, the reasoning wins. Use "High priority" as the main lens, "Medium" as support and "Avoid" as hard constraints. The "common answers" are what pages of this type often do; check them against your reasoning and don't copy them.

> **Order of authority:** `ethics.md` (hard limit) > `design-brief.md` > `reasoning.md` (method for writing the brief) > `principles.md` and `page-types.md` (consulted afterwards). When two disagree, the higher one wins.

Practices and regulation 2024–2026. Principle IDs (P01–P31) are in `principles.md`.

## Legend

- 3C weight: Cap (capture) / Conf (confidence/trust) / Conv (conversion) on a 0–3 scale (3 = critical on this page). "Act" (activation/continuity) and "Mem" (memory) are added when they matter.
- Persuasion: High / Medium / Low / None (see `principles.md` §3).
- High / medium priority: principle IDs from `principles.md`.
- Avoid: principles that become counterproductive here, or prohibited tactics.
- Metric: main success indicator.

## A. ENTRY AND MARKETING

### A1 · Landing page
- User's job: decide within seconds whether this specific offer is for them and take a single step.
- Weight: Cap 3 / Conf 2 / Conv 3. Persuasion: High.
- High priority: P01, P04, P05, P07, P16, P19, P03.
- Medium priority: P11, P14, P15, P06.
- Avoid: full global navigation (leaks), multiple competing CTAs, fake P24.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): headline that repeats the promise of the ad (message match); real product visible in the hero; a single action repeated throughout the scroll; objections block before the final CTA; specific social proof near the CTA.
- Difference from Home: the landing page serves ONE audience and ONE action; Home routes to several.
- Real example: Duolingo/ChatGPT/Shopify turn the landing page into the product itself (value before sign-up, P21).
- Metric: conversion rate by traffic source.

### A2 · Home
- Job: understand what the company is and find my path (new buyer, returning customer, press, candidate).
- Weight: Cap 3 / Conf 3 / Conv 1–2. Persuasion: High.
- High priority: P01, P02, P12, P09, P07.
- Medium priority: P08, P11, P10, P03.
- Avoid: treating it as a single-action landing page; abstract heroes that don't say what you do; claims without proof.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): clear hero (what + what for) + quantified proof (e.g. Stripe: volume processed, uptime linked to status, subscriptions managed); entry points by profile or use case; "why" at a second level.
- Real example: Stripe ("Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue" + verifiable figures); Linear (single sentence + real interface).
- Metric: click-through to key routes; abandonment in the first 10–30 s.

### A3 · About us
- Job: decide whether I trust the people behind it.
- Weight: Cap 1 / Conf 3 / Conv 1. Persuasion: Medium.
- High priority: P08, P12, P07.
- Medium priority: P11, P02.
- Avoid: generic phrases ("passionate team", "innovative solutions"); stock photos; mission without facts.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): the company's "why" + concrete proof (founding date, real names and faces, figures, physical address, investors/customers if public); story with a specific moment, not a summary.
- Specificity in the object ("in 2019, in a garage in Bilbao...") > adjectives.
- Metric: About us visits that continue to contact/sign-up.

### A4 · Plans and pricing
- Job: choose a plan without fear of getting it wrong or overpaying.
- Weight: Cap 1 / Conf 3 / Conv 3. Persuasion: High (but honest).
- High priority: P13, P10, P22, P23, P14, P07.
- Medium priority: P11, P04, P20.
- Avoid: hiding the monthly price behind the annual one; a "Most popular" that isn't; decoys with no real sense; "Contact sales" on every plan without need; small print on renewals.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): 2–4 plans, one recommended with justification ("for teams of 5–50"); annual/monthly toggle with savings as a figure; scannable comparison table; FAQ that defuses objections (cancellation, plan changes, taxes, data when downgrading); total price visible including per-user.
- Evidence: Economist/Ariely on decoys (P22, with caution); Baymard on unexpected costs (P13).
- Metric: conversion to trial/paid and plan mix; billing support tickets (should go down).

### A5 · Coming soon / Waitlist
- Job: decide whether to sign up and whether it's worth sharing.
- Weight: Cap 3 / Conf 2 / Conv 2. Persuasion: High.
- High priority: P01, P06, P26, P11.
- Medium priority: P08, P15, P24 (real places only).
- Avoid: fake counters; invented dates that later move; asking for more than an email.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): concrete, repeatable promise in one sentence; one field; thank-you page with queue position and a link to move up; access in waves.
- Real example: Robinhood got around 10,000 sign-ups on day one, ~50,000 in the first week and close to 1 M in the first year with a queue where referring moved you up (Prefinery / KickoffLabs).
- Metric: sign-up rate; % of sign-ups arriving via referral; list-to-user conversion at launch.

## B. CONTENT AND NAVIGATION

### B1 · Catalogue / Listing
- Job: narrow many options down to a few candidates.
- Weight: Cap 1 / Conf 2 / Conv 2. Persuasion: Medium.
- High priority: P10, P09, P31, P13, P03.
- Medium priority: P11 (average rating and number of reviews), P07.
- Avoid: misleading "from" prices; sponsored ordering without disclosure (the CMA required sponsored results to be distinguished); infinite scroll without being able to return to your position.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): faceted filters with counts; default order explained; final price visible; dominant image on the card; preserve filters and position when coming back.
- Metric: clicks to product page; filter usage; time to add to cart.

### B2 · Category / Collection
- Job: understand what's in this universe and whether it's for me.
- Weight: Cap 2 / Conf 1 / Conv 2. Persuasion: Medium.
- High priority: P10, P09, P31.
- Medium priority: P08 (editorial curation), P11 (real best sellers), P07.
- Difference from Catalogue: more editorial and inspiration-oriented; can tell a collection story; the catalogue is search and filtering.
- Avoid: giant banners that push products off the first screen on mobile.
- Metric: navigation depth; clicks to product page.

### B3 · Product page / Detail
- Job: be convinced that this specific product solves my need and know what it will really cost me.
- Weight: Cap 1 / Conf 3 / Conv 3. Persuasion: High.
- High priority: P31, P11, P13, P14, P07, P03.
- Medium priority: P24 (real stock only), P22 (variants), P17.
- Avoid: fake "only 2 left"; reviews filtered to positives only; hidden delivery times.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): gallery with thumbnails, zoom and in-scale photo (Baymard: 56% start with images; 42% try to infer size); reviews with distribution and filters; total cost and delivery date near the button; returns policy next to the CTA.
- Metric: add to cart; returns due to "not what I expected".

### B4 · Search results
- Job: quickly find what I already know I want.
- Weight: Cap 0 / Conf 1 / Conv 2. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P09, P03, P10, P29 (no results).
- Medium priority: P07 (repeat the query and number of results), P17 (correct typos).
- Avoid: "0 results" page with no way out; disguised sponsored results.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): autocomplete; typo tolerance; no results → suggestions, nearby categories, contact.
- Metric: rate of searches with no results; clicks on top positions; refinements.

### B5 · Map
- Job: decide by location, comparing place and price at the same time.
- Weight: Cap 1 / Conf 2 / Conv 2. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P09, P10 (clustering), P13 (total price on the pin).
- Medium priority: P31 (photo on card), P03.
- Avoid: pins with a base price that isn't final; a map that traps scrolling on mobile.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): list and map in sync; "search this area"; card on pin tap.
- Real example: Airbnb shows the total price with fees in results since April 2025 (P13).
- Metric: pin interaction → product page.

### B6 · Calendar / Schedule
- Job: find and book a slot without errors.
- Weight: Cap 0 / Conf 2 / Conv 2. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P17 (conflicts, time zones), P23 (detected time zone), P09, P10.
- Medium priority: P24 (real availability), P19.
- Avoid: fake availability urgency; time ambiguity.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): show only genuinely available slots; explicit time zone; confirmation with add-to-calendar.
- Metric: completed bookings; cancellations/rescheduling due to errors.

## C. COMMERCE

### C1 · Cart
- Job: review and confirm that what I'm buying and what I'll pay is correct.
- Weight: Cap 0 / Conf 3 / Conv 3. Persuasion: Medium-low.
- High priority: P13, P14, P19, P17.
- Medium priority: P23 (save for later), P29 (useful empty cart).
- Avoid: artificial timers; automatically added extras; hiding shipping until the end.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): estimated total with shipping and taxes; returns policy visible; edit quantities without reloading; "checkout as guest" visible already here.
- Data: 40% of abandonments (excluding "just browsing") due to extra costs; 12% due to not being able to calculate the total (Baymard).
- Metric: cart → checkout step.

### C2 · Checkout / Payment
- Job: pay without fear and without effort.
- Weight: Cap 0 / Conf 3 / Conv 3. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P19, P13, P12, P17, P18, P30.
- Medium priority: P23 (most common shipping preselected), P09.
- Avoid: mandatory account creation (18% abandonment); pre-ticked extras; distracting navigation; price changes between steps.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): 12–14 form elements (Baymard); guest by default; express payment methods; security seals next to the card field (19% abandon due to distrust); inline validation; order summary always visible.
- EU obligation: authentication and payment are in scope of the European Accessibility Act (since 28-06-2025).
- Metric: checkout conversion; errors per field.

### C3 · Order confirmation
- Job: know that everything went well and what happens next.
- Weight: Conf 3 / Mem 3 / Act 2. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P28, P07 (order number, delivery date), P12.
- Medium priority: P27 (useful next step), P15, P26.
- Avoid: aggressive upsells that spoil the ending; ending without tracking information.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): explicit confirmation + what happens now + when + where to see it; create an account with a single field (password or passkey) using the data already given; proportionate celebration moment.
- Metric: "was my order placed?" tickets (should be ~0); post-purchase account creation.

### C4 · Order history
- Job: sort it out myself (track, return, reorder).
- Weight: Conf 3 / Act 2. Persuasion: None-low.
- High priority: P17, P14, P09.
- Medium priority: P10, P13 (clear amounts and refunds).
- Avoid: phone-only returns when the purchase was online.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): status per order in human language; self-service return/cancel; "order again".
- Metric: % of issues resolved without contacting support.

## D. AUTHENTICATION

### D1 · Sign-up
- Job: get access without feeling I'm giving away my data.
- Weight: Conf 2 / Conv 3 / Act 2. Persuasion: Medium.
- High priority: P21, P19, P30, P15.
- Medium priority: P11, P14 ("no card"), P04.
- Avoid: asking for phone, company or job title without need; passwords with hidden rules; sign-up before any value if the product allows it.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): value before sign-up (Duolingo: ~20% more DAU by delaying sign-up); social login + passkey + email; explain why you ask for each piece of data.
- Metric: sign-up rate; subsequent activation rate (not just sign-up).

### D2 · Login
- Job: get in fast.
- Weight: Conv 2 / Act 3. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P30, P09, P17, P23.
- Medium priority: P03.
- Avoid: CAPTCHA by default; blocking password pasting; error messages that blame.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): passkeys with autofill (93% success vs 78% password+MFA, FIDO 2026); magic link; "remember me"; generic error message for security but with a clear path to recovery.
- Metric: sign-in success; time to get in; access tickets.

### D3 · Password recovery
- Job: get back in without stress.
- Weight: Conf 3 / Mem 2. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P17, P28, P30.
- Avoid: revealing whether an email exists (security); links that expire without warning.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): same message whether or not the account exists; link with stated expiry; at the end, sign in automatically and offer a passkey so it doesn't happen again (positive ending, P28).
- Metric: % of recoveries completed.

### D4 · Verification (email / 2FA)
- Job: complete a security step without getting lost.
- Weight: Conf 2 / Conv 2. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P19, P17, P09.
- Medium priority: P26 (step X of Y), P23.
- Avoid: codes that can't be pasted; resend without a visible timer.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): code autofill (one-time-code); resend with countdown; open mail client; explain why it's needed in one line.
- Metric: verification rate and time to verify.

### D5 · Onboarding
- Job: reach the first useful result as soon as possible.
- Weight: Act 3 / Conv 2. Persuasion: Medium.
- High priority: P27, P26, P21, P20, P29.
- Medium priority: P25 (with an escape valve), P04, P05.
- Avoid: 10-screen tours before using the product; asking for permissions (notifications) before their value is understood.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): goal question (Duolingo asks for motivation and daily goal); checklist with the first step already done; templates; push toward the activation metric (Slack: 2,000 messages → 93% kept using it).
- Metric: time to value; % reaching the activation action; D1/D7 retention.

## E. USER AND ACCOUNT

### E1 · Profile
- Job: control how others see me and complete what's needed.
- Weight: Conf 2 / Act 2. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P12 (visibility control), P26, P17.
- Avoid: progress bars that demand unnecessary data.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): "how others see you" preview; progress only toward fields that add value for the user.
- Metric: profiles complete in the fields that matter.

### E2 · Settings
- Job: find and change a specific option without breaking anything.
- Weight: Conf 2 / Act 2. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P09, P10, P23, P17.
- Medium priority: P05 (advanced hidden).
- Avoid: defaults that favour the company against the user (minimum privacy, data sharing on).
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): grouping by task; settings search; autosave with visible confirmation; confirmation only for destructive actions.
- Metric: "where do I change...?" tickets.

### E3 · Account and billing
- Job: understand what I pay, when, and be able to change or cancel it.
- Weight: Conf 3 / Conv 1 (upgrade) / Mem 2. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P14, P13, P07, P17.
- Medium priority: P20 (upgrade on hitting a real limit), P28 (dignified exit).
- Avoid: "Iliad"-style flows (Amazon: $2.5 B with the FTC in 2025); confirmshaming; hiding the renewal date; looping retention offers.
- Regulation: the proposed Digital Fairness Act (EU, 11-11-2026) requires a simple, unambiguous cancellation option and reminders before trials end and before renewals.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): next charge date and amount always visible; cancel in as many steps as it took to sign up; a single optional retention offer; downloadable invoices.
- Metric: voluntary cancellations vs chargebacks/complaints; billing tickets.

### E4 · Notifications
- Job: decide what gets to interrupt me.
- Weight: Act 3 / Conf 2. Persuasion: None-low.
- High priority: P20, P23, P10.
- Avoid: asking for permission in the first second; notifications without granular control; "notifications" that are advertising.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): permission in context (after an action that justifies it); control by type and channel; daily digest as an alternative.
- Metric: permission acceptance rate; opt-out rate.

## F. INTERACTION AND CREATION

### F1 · Form / Content creation
- Job: finish without losing what I wrote.
- Weight: Conv 3 / Conf 2. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P19, P17, P26, P05.
- Medium priority: P04, P18.
- Avoid: ambiguous optional fields (Expedia case: $12 M/year); validating on the first keystroke; wiping content after an error.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): single column; visible labels; numbered steps if long; autosave; errors next to the field, with a solution.
- Metric: completion rate; errors per field; abandonment per step.

### F2 · Editor
- Job: create fluidly and without fear of breaking things.
- Weight: Act 3. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P09 (standard shortcuts), P17 (undo > confirm), P03 (latency).
- Medium priority: P29 (templates in an empty document), P05.
- Avoid: constant confirmation dialogs; losing work; reinventing universal shortcuts.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): autosave with visible status; version history; commands ("/" or palette).
- Metric: sessions with completed creation; reported lost work.

### F3 · Messages / Chat
- Job: communicate with certainty of delivery.
- Weight: Act 3 / Conf 2. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P03, P17 (sent/failed/retry status), P09.
- Medium priority: P29 (empty conversation with a starter suggestion), P27 (if it's a collaborative product).
- Avoid: read receipts without user control; losing drafts.
- Metric: messages sent successfully; response time.

### F4 · Feed / Activity
- Job: catch up with what's relevant.
- Weight: Act 3 / Mem 2. Persuasion: Medium (with ethical limits).
- High priority: P10 (relevance), P11, P20.
- Medium priority: P25 (streaks with a valve), P06 (moderate).
- Avoid: infinite scroll with no perceptible end and compulsive mechanics; the proposed Digital Fairness Act (EU, 2026) targets addictive design and the protection of minors.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): "you're all caught up" marker; filters by type; chronological order available.
- Metric: useful return (actions after reading), not just screen time.

## G. MANAGEMENT AND ADMINISTRATION

### G1 · Dashboard
- Job: know within seconds what needs my attention.
- Weight: Act 3 / Conf 2. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P01 (the main question answered at the top), P10, P07 (figures with comparison: vs previous period, vs target), P29.
- Medium priority: P03, P09.
- Avoid: walls of charts with no hierarchy; vanity metrics.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): actionable items at the top; empty states with a path to the first data; trends with context.
- Metric: time to action; recurring use.

### G2 · Analytics / Reports
- Job: understand and communicate what happened.
- Weight: Conf 3. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P07, P17, P09.
- Medium priority: P10, P29.
- Avoid: truncated axes that exaggerate; hiding the period or the metric definition (misleading anchoring).
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): each metric's definition accessible; export; compare periods.
- Metric: reports exported/shared; support questions about definitions.

### G3 · Admin panel
- Job: operate safely and quickly, often in volume.
- Weight: Conf 3. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P17 (strong confirmations for irreversible actions), P09, P12 (audit log).
- Medium priority: P10 (high density acceptable for experts).
- Difference: here expert efficiency matters more than ease for novices; dense tables and shortcuts are correct.
- Metric: operational errors; time per task.

### G4 · User / Team management
- Job: invite, assign permissions and remove access without errors.
- Weight: Act 3 / Conf 3. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P27 (inviting teammates is often the activation action in collaborative products), P17, P12.
- Medium priority: P10, P13 (cost per seat visible when inviting).
- Avoid: surprise charges when adding users.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): roles explained in plain language; permission preview; cost before confirming.
- Metric: accepted invitations; permission incidents.

## H. BLOG AND RESOURCES

### H1 · Article list
- Job: decide what to read.
- Weight: Cap 3. Persuasion: Medium.
- High priority: P07 (specific titles), P06 (honest curiosity), P04 (reading time).
- Medium priority: P10 (categories), P31 (image only if it informs).
- Avoid: clickbait that doesn't pay off (breaks trust in all future titles).
- Metric: clicks to article; full reads.

### H2 · Article / Post
- Job: get the answer or the idea, fast.
- Weight: Cap 3 / Conf 2 / Conv 1. Persuasion: Medium.
- High priority: P01 (answer at the top, inverted pyramid), P05, P07, P12 (author, date, update).
- Medium priority: P06, P04, P15 (CTA after delivering value).
- Writing: each paragraph earns the next; cut what is interesting but irrelevant; short sentences; 10-year-old test.
- Avoid: filler introductions; pop-ups before delivering value.
- Metric: scroll depth; contextual CTA conversion.

### H3 · Documentation
- Job: solve a specific technical task.
- Weight: Act 3 / Conf 3. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P01 (task-oriented), P09, P03 (search), P07 (runnable code examples).
- Medium priority: P12 (version, date), P17.
- Real example: Stripe's docs front page is almost marketing-free: explore guides and examples to integrate.
- Avoid: sales tone; incomplete examples.
- Metric: searches with no results; time to first integration.

## I. SUPPORT AND CONTACT

### I1 · Contact
- Job: reach a person and know when they'll reply.
- Weight: Conf 3 / Conv 2. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P19, P12 (real address and channels), P04 (expected response time).
- Medium priority: P17.
- Avoid: long forms; hiding contact behind a chatbot with no way out.
- Metric: completed submissions; first response within the promised time.

### I2 · FAQ / Help
- Job: resolve the doubt without contacting anyone.
- Weight: Conf 3. Persuasion: None-low.
- High priority: P10 (search, grouping), P07, P01.
- Pre-empt objections: the pricing FAQ is a conversion tool.
- Metric: ticket deflection; searches with no answer.

### I3 · Support centre / Tickets
- Job: know the status of my problem.
- Weight: Conf 3 / Mem 3. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P17 (visible status), P28 (close with clear resolution), P04 (timeframes).
- Avoid: closing tickets without confirming resolution.
- Metric: satisfaction at close; reopenings.

## J. LEGAL
(No persuasion. Trust is earned through clarity.)

### J1 · Terms and conditions
- High priority: P01 (plain-language summary at the top), P18, P12 (effective date, change history).
- Avoid: silent changes; important clauses (renewal, cancellation) only here.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): layered summary + full text + index.

### J2 · Privacy policy
- High priority: P01, P12, P18.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): layers (what data, for what, with whom, for how long, your rights and how to exercise them) + direct links to exercise them.

### J3 · Cookie policy (and banner)
- High priority: P23 (no consent by default), P12.
- Avoid: rejecting harder than accepting; pre-ticked boxes; disguised "legitimate interest".
- Context (not verified by search in this research; confirm before citing): European data protection authorities (e.g. the French CNIL in 2022) have fined banners where rejecting is costlier than accepting, and the Spanish AEPD's cookie guide reflects the same idea. Treat accept/reject parity on the first layer as a design requirement.

## K. STATES AND SYSTEM

### K1 · 404 error
- Weight: Conf 2 / Mem 2. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P17, P09, P29.
- Medium priority: P28 (a touch of humour only if the brand allows it and it doesn't get in the way).
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): human language without blame ("This page doesn't exist or has moved"), search box, links to the most visited pages; log 404s to redirect.

### K2 · 500 error
- Weight: Conf 3 / Mem 3. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P17, P28, P12 (link to status page).
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): own the error ("it's our fault"), what to do now, preserve what the user was doing, when to retry. NN/g: in total failure, novelty (e.g. Twitter's "Fail Whale") can soften frustration.

### K3 · Maintenance
- Weight: Conf 3. Persuasion: None.
- High priority: P04 (specific return time with time zone), P12 (status), P14 (what happens to orders/data).
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): scheduled advance notice; alternative if available; "notify me" subscription.

### K4 · Empty state
- Weight: Act 3. Persuasion: Low.
- High priority: P29, P27, P26.
- Common answers (check against your reasoning, don't copy): what this is + why it's empty + main action + example/template. Distinguish types: first use, no results, cleared by the user (celebrate: "all caught up"), loading error (don't show "no data" if it's actually still loading — NN/g flags this as a common failure).

### K5 · Success / Thank-you page
- Weight: Mem 3 / Act 3. Persuasion: Medium.
- High priority: P28, P27 (next step), P15.
- Medium priority: P11 (referrals), P26.
- Real example: Robinhood turned the thank-you page into its viral engine (queue position + personal link).
- Avoid: dead end; offers that contradict what was just bought.
